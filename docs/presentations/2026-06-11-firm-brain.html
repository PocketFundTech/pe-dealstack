Firm Brain

Today our AI works one deal at a time. Firm Brain makes it remember everything the firm has ever learned — and put it to work on every new deal.

Compounding intelligence Survives team turnover A moat that grows over time
The problem we're not solving yet

A firm's edge is its memory.
Right now, that memory leaks.

Knowledge dies per deal

Everything we learn lives inside one deal. Open a new one and the AI starts from a blank page — even if we've seen the exact same pattern before.

It walks out the door

PE/M&A has high turnover. When an associate leaves, the "I remember we passed on that for a reason" knowledge leaves with them.

Every diligence restarts

Partners re-derive the same insights deal after deal. The firm's pattern-recognition never compounds into an asset it owns.

The thing PE firms actually sell — judgment across many deals — is the one thing our product doesn't capture.

The idea

An AI that keeps a living notebook of the whole firm.

  • 1
    After every deal, document, and call, the AI distills what matters into small, plain-English notes.
  • 2
    Notes about companies, people, and theses link to each other — building a map of everything the firm knows.
  • 3
    On any new deal, the AI reads the relevant notes first — so it shows up already knowing your history.
Why this works

It's a proven pattern — the same "second brain" idea (Karpathy's LLM-wiki, Obsidian-style linked notes) that powers the best AI memory systems. We make it firm-grade: private, permissioned, and built on data we already have.

The moment that sells it

"It remembered something I'd forgotten."

Firm Brain · on opening a new deal Heads up — you've evaluated 3 comparable vertical-SaaS targets. You passed on Acme (churn over 3%/mo — your thesis bar is 2%). This new CIM shows 3.4%/mo churn and the founder is the same profile. Want the side-by-side?

That sentence is the product. It turns a tool people use into a system the firm can't operate without — and a new associate becomes "senior" on day one because they inherit the firm brain.

Why this is lucrative

The longer they use it, the more it's worth — and the harder it is to leave.
